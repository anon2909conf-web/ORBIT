§ 1

Abstract

Few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL) requires incorporating novel classes from small support sets while preserving accuracy on previously learned classes, with the backbone frozen after the base session. The base-session embedding geometry therefore bounds the achievable accuracy in every subsequent session, yet base training does not optimise this geometry for prototype-based expansion. Existing feature-augmentation methods apply a single fixed procedure across datasets, although the effective dimensionality of base-class features varies substantially between datasets.


To overcome these issues, we propose ORthonormal Basis Incremental Representation Tuning (ORBIT), which computes the participation ratio of the base-class feature covariance in one forward pass, prior to any gradient update, and uses it to select between two training regimes. In the high-ratio (crowded) regime, convexly blended embeddings undergo a fixed random orthonormal rotation, batch-wise permutation mixing, inverse rotation and additive Gaussian noise, which introduces variation outside the convex hull of the original embeddings. In the low-ratio (separated) regime, only convex blending is applied, which preserves fine-grained discriminative structure. ORBIT introduces no additional parameters and stores no exemplars. It improves average incremental accuracy over the strongest prior method by 5.19 and 5.21 points on miniImageNet and CIFAR-100 (75.96% and 75.61%), and reaches 70.35% on CUB-200. Our experimental results on four additional datasets and forced-regime experiments show that disabling the rotation in the crowded regime reduces average accuracy by up to 5.89 points, emphasizing the efficacy of the proposed approach.

Framework Overview

Figure 1  ·  Overview of ORBIT. A single forward pass over the base data yields the crowding score (participation ratio of the feature covariance), which is compared with a fixed threshold to select the augmentation regime. The crowded regime applies blending followed by the orthonormal transformation; the separated regime applies blending alone.
§ 3

Core Method

01   Routing

Geometry-Driven Routing

One forward pass over the base data gives the participation ratio ρ of the feature covariance. With a fixed threshold τ = 46.6, ρ ≥ τ selects the crowded regime and ρ < τ the separated regime. The decision is made once, needs no labels, and adds no parameters.

02   Crowded

Orthonormal-Basis Augmentation

Paired embeddings are blended (α = 0.5), rotated by a fixed random orthonormal matrix, mixed with a permuted copy of the batch, rotated back and perturbed with Gaussian noise, which adds variation beyond the convex hull of the original embeddings.

03   Separated

Blending Only

Only the paired convex blending is applied, which preserves fine-grained discriminative structure. The backbone, the joint classification and contrastive loss, and the optimiser are identical in both regimes.

§ 5

Routing Analysis

The crowding score ρ separates the datasets into two groups with a single threshold (τ = 46.6). Forcing each dataset into the regime opposite to the routed one lowers average accuracy in every case. Forcing the separated regime on the crowded datasets costs 4.95 points on average (DTD, Food101, miniImageNet), whereas forcing the crowded regime on the separated datasets costs 0.53 points on average (GTSRB, STL10, CUB-200).

§ 7

Conclusion

We proposed ORBIT, a feature augmentation framework for FSCIL that adapts to the geometry of the base-class feature space instead of applying a fixed policy across datasets. A single forward pass over the base session determines whether aggressive diversification is needed, with no extra parameters, no stored exemplars and no manual tuning.


Geometry-aware routing, paired with orthonormal-basis augmentation, improves average incremental accuracy by +5.19 points on miniImageNet and +5.21 points on CIFAR-100 over the prior best. On CUB-200 the framework withholds the stronger branch and reaches 70.35%, which is 0.59 points below the best method, instead of perturbing an already well-structured representation. Forced-regime experiments show that the cost of an incorrect routing decision is asymmetric, with 4.95 points lost on average on crowded datasets against 0.53 points on separated datasets.
